# CoAssure — Niche Market Strategy & Staged Product Plan

## The Problem We Solve

Safety on site has become administrative theatre. Workers fill out Take 5s automatically, copy previous answers, and treat forms as obstacles. Management gets documentation that provides a false sense of assurance but lacks genuine safety intelligence.

Research shows that companies conducting daily interactive toolbox talks reduced their Total Recordable Incident Rate by 82% compared to monthly ones. But 48% of companies aren't confident their training prepares workers to do jobs safely, and 38% operate reactively rather than proactively. The tools exist — SafetyCulture, HammerTech, Procore — but the engagement doesn't. 93% of workers surveyed say their workgroup is currently at risk from a safety issue that is not being discussed.

The gap: **there is no intelligent, contextual, conversational safety partner at the point of risk — especially on the sites that need it most.**

The big Tier 1 projects have safety advisors on the ground, dedicated systems, and full-time compliance teams. But the vast majority of work doesn't happen on those sites. It happens on smaller and medium sites that change weekly. On service calls to client premises. In the field. On maintenance shutdowns. On projects that have safety requirements but not safety infrastructure.

Those workers and those sites are underserved. The paperwork requirements are the same, but the support is not.

---

## What We Are Building

**CoAssure is a voice-first safety assistant that makes it easy for workers and teams to think through hazards, fill out safety documents, run toolbox talks, and report what's happening on site — using conversation instead of clipboards.**

It works alongside the safety systems organisations already use (SafetyCulture, Procore, HammerTech, paper forms). It makes the data going into those systems better, the forms easier to complete, and the conversations more meaningful. On smaller and mid-size sites that don't have dedicated safety platforms, it can become the primary system.

**The psychological hook:** When you speak your plan out loud, you find the holes in it. Research confirms that interactive discussion produces dramatically better safety outcomes than passive form-filling — because speech forces deliberate thinking (System 2) instead of autopilot (System 1).

**The organisational hook:** The company defines the standards, procedures, and priorities. The AI translates those into natural conversation — not interrogation, not checklists. The output satisfies compliance requirements automatically.

**The practical hook:** It's faster than typing, works with gloves on, and fits into whatever safety system the client already uses.

## The Niche

### Target Market

**Workers and teams on sites that don't have dedicated safety infrastructure — changing job sites, small-to-medium projects, field service, maintenance, and sub-contractors working across multiple clients.**

This includes:

- **Small-to-medium construction projects** — residential builds, fit-outs, renovations, civil works that don't justify a full-time safety advisor
- **Field service teams** — HVAC, electrical, plumbing, elevator maintenance, telecommunications, utilities
- **Maintenance and shutdown crews** — moving between sites, high paperwork burden, tight schedules
- **Sub-contractors** — working on client sites with unfamiliar safety systems, different forms every week
- **Remote and regional workers** — surveyors, agronomists, infrastructure inspectors, utility crews
- **Lone workers** — the most isolated and least supported cohort

### Why This Niche

1. **Same compliance burden, less support.** These workers face the same Take 5, JSA, SWMS, and permit requirements as a Tier 1 site — but without the safety advisor standing next to them. The forms are identical. The help is not.
2. **Changing environments.** Unlike a fixed site with a stable hazard profile, these workers face new conditions constantly — different client premises, different projects, different weather, different trades working around them. Static checklists can't keep up.
3. **High form fatigue.** When workers complete the same paperwork across multiple sites, the repetition breeds autopilot. Research shows the brain automates familiar, repetitive tasks — this is neurology, not laziness.
4. **High sub-contractor density.** Sub-contractors are juggling one tool for inductions, another for training records, another for access control — and still relying on WhatsApp for everything else. 47% of enterprise contractors still rely on paper and spreadsheets for safety workflows.
5. **Practical technology fit.** These workers carry smartphones. They already use voice notes. The behaviour pattern exists — it just needs a purpose-built tool.
6. **Underserved by current platforms.** SafetyCulture, HammerTech, and Procore are designed for large projects with dedicated safety teams. When a foreman or subbie has to click through endless menus to submit a report, it gets skipped or delayed. These tools are powerful — but they're not designed for the worker in the van.

## The Core Experience

### Five Entry Points

CoAssure is not a single workflow. It has five distinct entry points that match different moments in the working day:

**1. The Pre-Start Talk-Through (Solo or Small Team)**

The worker (or a pair/small crew) is heading to a job or has just arrived. They press the mic button and talk through what they're facing.

> **Worker:** "At the Henderson Road site. Replacing the hot water unit in the plant room. Tight space, old copper pipes, the system was running this morning so it'll still be warm."

> **CoAssure:** "Tight space with warm pipes — scald risk on the disconnect. Are you giving it time to cool, or do you have a plan for that? Also, the old copper — any chance of asbestos lagging on those pipes?"

The system already knows: the weather (35°C — heat stress applies), the location (commercial building), and the company standard that manual handling above 25kg requires a two-person lift or mechanical assist.

**This is the hero feature for lone workers and small crews.** 30–60 seconds. Not a form. A conversation.

**2. The Facilitated Toolbox Talk (Team)**

A supervisor or team leader runs the morning pre-start briefing with their crew. CoAssure acts as the facilitator — or assists the person running it.

Two modes:

**Supervisor-led (AI assists):** The supervisor leads the discussion as normal. CoAssure listens, records, and prompts. When the conversation stalls or misses something critical, it interjects:

> **Supervisor:** "Right, we're pouring the slab today. Any hazards?"
> **Worker 1:** "Concrete burns. We've got the gloves and boots."
> **Worker 2:** "Pump truck's coming in at 9."
> **CoAssure:** "Good. One thing — it's 38 degrees today and you're pouring on an exposed slab. Heat stress is a real risk. What's the plan for shade breaks and hydration?"

The AI doesn't take over. It fills the gaps. It asks the question the supervisor didn't think of — or the one nobody wanted to raise.

**AI-facilitated (no supervisor available):** For small crews or remote teams where there's no dedicated supervisor for the pre-start. The AI runs the toolbox talk:

> **CoAssure:** "Morning. Three of you on site today — what's the main job?"
> **Worker 1:** "Trenching for the new conduit run."
> **CoAssure:** "Alright. Who's operating the excavator, and who's working in or near the trench?"

It guides the discussion, ensures everyone speaks, checks hazards against org standards, and captures the whole thing.

**In both modes:** The conversation is recorded and transcribed. CoAssure generates a structured toolbox talk record — attendees, topics discussed, hazards raised, controls confirmed, actions assigned — ready for the compliance file. The discussion itself satisfies the WHS Act's requirement for genuine worker consultation (Section 48), which demands workers have the opportunity to express views and contribute to decisions — not just sign a sheet.

**3. The Form Assistant (Scan, Talk, Fill)**

Workers constantly encounter forms they need to fill out — their own company's Take 5, a client's JSA, a site SWMS, a permit to work. CoAssure helps them complete any form faster and better.

**Three ways in:**

**Scan a QR code:** The form (paper or posted at a site) has a QR code. Worker scans it. CoAssure loads the form structure and walks them through it conversationally — one section at a time, in plain language, with context-aware prompts.

**Photograph a paper form:** Worker takes a photo of an unfamiliar form. CoAssure reads it (OCR), identifies the fields, and walks them through it: "This is asking about your hazards for today. What are you seeing on site?"

**Open from the app:** Worker selects from their organisation's template library (or a commonly used industry form) and talks through it.

In every case, the AI doesn't just transcribe — it challenges. If the worker says "No hazards," it pushes back: "You mentioned it was raining earlier — is the work area wet? And have you checked for overhead services?"

**The output:** A completed, structured form — either saved in CoAssure, exported as PDF, pasted into the client's system, or pushed via API to SafetyCulture/Procore/HammerTech.

**On free-tier outputs, the form includes a branded footer:**

> *This safety assessment was completed using CoAssure. [View full context: weather, location, risk prompts, and worker responses →]*

The link goes to a read-only page showing the depth of the assessment. When a client's safety team clicks that link and sees weather data, GPS, a full transcript, and the AI prompts that challenged the worker's thinking — they see something no ticked checkbox has ever shown them. **That footer is the sales funnel.**

**4. Easy Reporting — Near-Misses, Hazards, Observations, Good Catches**

The biggest gap in safety data is the stuff that almost happened. Near-misses are the leading indicators that predict serious incidents — but they're massively underreported because the reporting process is painful. Typing a detailed description into a tiny phone keyboard while standing on a job site is nobody's idea of a good time.

CoAssure makes it effortless:

> **Worker:** (taps the "Report" button) "Nearly got cleaned up by the forklift backing out of the warehouse. Driver didn't see me. No spotter. I had to jump out of the way."

> **CoAssure:** "That's a serious near-miss. Were you hurt at all? And was the reversing alarm working on the forklift?"

> **Worker:** "Nah, I'm fine. Alarm was going but I had my earplugs in so I didn't hear it until the last second. That's probably the real issue."

CoAssure generates a structured near-miss report:

```
NEAR-MISS REPORT — 17 Feb 2026, 10:23 AM
Reporter: Dave Mitchell | Site: Industrial Park, Unit 7
Category: Vehicle/Pedestrian Interaction (Severity: High)

WHAT HAPPENED:
Forklift reversed from warehouse without spotter.
Worker in path, had to take evasive action. No injury.

ROOT FACTORS IDENTIFIED:
- No spotter for reversing forklift
- Hearing protection masked reversing alarm
- Pedestrian/vehicle traffic separation unclear

WORKER SUGGESTION:
Review hearing protection vs. situational awareness
in forklift operating areas.
```

This same flow works for:
- **Hazard reports** — "There's a broken rung on the ladder at Building C"
- **Good catches** — "Found the gas bottle was leaking before we connected it"
- **General observations** — "The new blokes don't seem to know about the exclusion zone near the crane"
- **End-of-job reflections** — "Job's done. One thing to flag for next time..."

Every report is GPS-tagged, timestamped, and routed to the right people. Voice makes it 75–90% faster than typing. The friction drops so low that reporting becomes a habit instead of a chore.

**5. The Post-Job Reflection**

At the end of a job or a shift, the worker records a quick voice note:

> **CoAssure:** "How'd the job go? Anything worth noting for next time?"

> **Worker:** "Went fine. The access to the roof was dodgier than expected — the ladder's not secured properly. And the client said there might be more work next week, so whoever comes back should know about the ladder."

This becomes: a close-out record, a hazard flag (unsecured ladder), and a knowledge transfer note for the next worker sent to that site. Information that would normally be lost in the van ride home is now captured and stored.

## The Intelligence Engine

CoAssure does not ask generic questions. It pulls in as much context as possible to make every conversation specific to this worker, this site, this moment.

### Data Sources

| Source | What It Provides | How It's Used |
|---|---|---|
| **GPS / Location** | Site address, proximity to known hazards, site history | "You're at the same address where someone reported an unsecured ladder last week. Worth checking." |
| **Weather API (BOM)** | Temperature, wind, rain, UV, forecast | "Wind gusts are at 45km/h. That's above the limit for the EWP — have you got an alternative plan?" |
| **Time of Day / Day of Week** | Fatigue patterns, shift timing | "It's Friday afternoon — statistically the highest-risk window. Double-check your isolations." |
| **Organisation Standards** | Golden Rules, SOPs, SWMS templates, safety bulletins, incident reports | "Your company's confined space procedure requires atmospheric testing before entry. Have you done that?" |
| **Project Context** | Current phase, active zones, trades on site, known conditions | "This site is in commissioning phase — watch for live electrical testing today." |
| **Crowd-Sourced Reports** | Other workers' voice notes and hazard reports from the same location | "Three workers mentioned 'slippery access road' here this morning." |
| **Industry Feeds** | Safety alerts, product recalls, regulatory updates, seasonal hazards | "SafeWork issued an alert yesterday about faulty harness buckles from that manufacturer." |
| **Form Context** | The specific form being filled out, the client's requirements | "This JSA asks about exclusion zones — is the crane swing radius marked on the ground?" |

### How Standards Are Applied

The organisation uploads their documents — SOPs, SWMS templates, safety bulletins, incident reports, Golden Rules, toolbox talk content. The system indexes and understands them.

When a worker speaks, the AI cross-references what they've said against the organisation's rules. It does not recite policy. It translates policy into natural prompts:

- **Policy says:** "All excavation work requires Dial Before You Dig confirmation."
- **AI says:** "You mentioned trenching — have you got the DBYD clearance for this address?"

- **Policy says:** "Working at heights above 2m requires a completed SWMS and harness inspection."
- **AI says:** "You're heading up on the scaffold — when did you last check the harness tag?"

The organisation controls what matters. The AI controls how it's asked.

### How Forms Are Understood

When a worker scans a QR code or photographs a form, CoAssure:

1. Identifies the form type (Take 5, JSA, SWMS, permit, inspection checklist)
2. Maps the fields to its understanding of safety documentation
3. Walks the worker through each section conversationally — using progressive disclosure (one section at a time, revealing detail only when relevant)
4. Cross-references responses against weather, location, org standards, and crowd-sourced data
5. Challenges gaps ("You said 'no hazards' but you're working near an open excavation in the rain — want to reconsider?")
6. Generates the completed form in the required format

For common platforms (SafetyCulture, Procore, HammerTech), the output can be pushed directly via API. For everything else, it generates a PDF or structured text that the worker pastes in.

### Worker Profiles — Respecting Experience and Training

CoAssure does not treat everyone the same. A 20-year electrician with a spotless safety record and current confined space certification should not be spoken to like a first-year apprentice. That's disrespectful, and it's the fastest way to lose trust with the people who matter most.

**Every worker has a profile that shapes how the AI interacts with them:**

**Training & Certifications:**
Workers register their training easily — voice or photo of a certificate. Working at Heights, Confined Space, EWP, First Aid, White Card, trade licences, client-specific inductions. The system tracks what's current, what's expiring, and what's missing.

- If a worker has a current Confined Space cert, the AI doesn't explain what atmospheric testing is. It says: "You know the drill — have you done the atmo test?"
- If a worker does NOT have the cert, the AI flags it: "This looks like it might need a confined space entry — do you have the training for that? Worth checking with your supervisor."

**Experience:**
Years in the trade, years with the company, types of work regularly performed. The AI uses this to calibrate its tone and depth:

- **Experienced worker:** The AI is brief, peer-to-peer. "One thing — the hoses." It trusts them to know the context.
- **Newer worker:** The AI is more thorough, more explanatory. "The hoses on these pumps can fail under pressure — check the couplings before you pressurise." Still not patronising, but more supportive.

**Safety Record:**
A personal safety engagement score built from their CoAssure interactions — not a punishment metric, but a recognition one:

- Conversation quality (specific hazards vs. one-word answers)
- Near-miss and hazard reports filed
- Good catches
- Toolbox talk contributions
- Training currency

Workers with strong records get acknowledged: "You've been flagging good stuff lately — keep it up." Workers with declining engagement get a gentle nudge, not a warning.

**How it affects the AI's behaviour:**

| Worker Profile | AI Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Experienced, current training, strong record | Brief, peer-to-peer. Trusts their judgment. Focuses on what's unusual about today (weather, location, crowd-sourced data) rather than basics. |
| Experienced, training expiring | Reminds about expiry naturally: "Heads up — your confined space cert expires next month." |
| Mid-level, good record | Balanced. Checks key controls, asks 1–2 challenge questions. |
| Newer worker, limited training | More supportive, more explanatory. Walks through steps. Suggests consulting senior workers or supervisors for unfamiliar tasks. |
| Any worker, unfamiliar site/task | Regardless of experience, if the task or site is new to this worker, the AI provides more context. Experience in one area doesn't mean expertise in another. |

**The principle:** Treat people the way an experienced colleague would — with respect for what they know, awareness of what they might not, and a focus on what's different about today.

**Registration is easy:**
- Voice: "I've got my confined space, working at heights, and EWP. All current."
- Photo: Snap a photo of the certificate. CoAssure reads it (OCR), extracts the details, and logs it.
- Manual: Quick form in the app — select training type, enter date, done.
- Org-managed: For enterprise clients, training records can be synced from the company's training management system.

**Expiry tracking:**
The system knows when certifications expire and incorporates this naturally:
- 30 days out: "Your EWP ticket expires on the 15th. Worth getting that renewal booked."
- Expired: "Your working at heights cert expired last month. You might need to sort that before this job."
- On the dashboard: Managers see a training currency view across their team — who's current, who's expiring, who has gaps.

## What Managers and Organisations Get That They Don't Get Today

This section addresses the specific outcomes CoAssure delivers that traditional systems cannot.

### Outcomes That Would Not Otherwise Exist

**1. The near-miss flood.**
When reporting takes 30 seconds of voice instead of 5 minutes of typing, near-miss reports increase 5–10x. Each near-miss is a leading indicator — a preview of a serious incident that hasn't happened yet. Traditional systems capture a fraction of these because the reporting friction is too high. CoAssure removes the friction. Suddenly management has a real-time stream of early warning signals that never existed before.

**2. The pattern nobody saw.**
When thousands of voice conversations are transcribed and analysed, the AI can identify patterns that no human reviewer would catch:
- "Workers at the Riverside site mention 'poor lighting' three times more often than any other site — but nobody has filed a formal hazard report about it."
- "Friday afternoon sessions across all sites show a 40% drop in hazard specificity compared to Monday mornings — fatigue is measurable in the data."
- "Excavation crews mention 'soft ground' 60% more often after rain, but the SWMS for those jobs doesn't reference ground conditions at all."

These are discoveries, not reports. They emerge from the data because the data is richer than anything a form-based system collects.

**3. The consultation evidence.**
Under the WHS Act (Section 48), genuine consultation requires workers to express views and contribute to decisions. A signed attendance sheet proves someone was present — it does not prove consultation occurred. A CoAssure toolbox talk transcript shows every question asked, every hazard raised by a worker, every control discussed, and every decision made. If a regulator or a court asks "did you consult with workers about the risks?", the transcript is a complete answer.

**4. The sub-contractor window.**
For the first time, the organisation can see what sub-contractors were thinking before they started work — without buying them software or mandating a platform. When a subbie uses CoAssure (free tier) to fill out the client's form, the branded link shows the full conversation. The site safety team sees: the subbie considered these hazards, was challenged on these gaps, and confirmed these controls. That visibility simply didn't exist before.

**5. The site knowledge base.**
Every pre-start, every reflection, every hazard report at a given location builds a cumulative picture of that site. When a new worker is sent there six months later, CoAssure can say: "Previous workers at this address flagged unsecured roof access, noisy plant room making communication difficult, and limited parking for work vehicles." The site has a memory — one that doesn't rely on a single person's recollection.

**6. The training signal.**
When the data shows that 70% of workers skip mentioning manual handling controls, or that apprentices consistently miss electrical isolation steps, the organisation has a specific, evidence-based training need — not a generic annual refresher topic. The conversations reveal exactly where the gaps are, by trade, by experience level, by site type.

## Design Principles

1. **The "Dirty Glove" Test.** If the worker has to take off their gloves to use it, it has failed. Everything must be voice-controllable with minimal screen interaction.

2. **30 Seconds, Not 10 Minutes.** The pre-start conversation should take 30–60 seconds. A form filling session should be faster than doing it by hand. Respect the worker's time.

3. **Challenge, Don't Interrogate.** The AI asks 1–2 pointed questions. It does not run through a 20-item checklist verbally. Quality over quantity.

4. **Empower, Don't Surveil.** This is a tool that helps the worker. The tone is always: "I'm here to help you think" — never "I'm watching you."

5. **Assist First, Replace Later.** CoAssure works alongside existing systems. It makes the data going into SafetyCulture, Procore, or HammerTech better. On sites that don't need those platforms, CoAssure becomes the system. The worker's experience is the same either way.

6. **Organisation Sets the Standard, AI Sets the Tone.** The company defines what matters (rules, priorities, hazards). The AI decides how to raise it naturally in conversation. Workers never feel like they're being read a policy document.

7. **Offline is Not an Edge Case.** Many workers operate in basements, tunnels, rural areas, and underground. Offline must work seamlessly — record locally, process and sync later.

8. **Any Form, Any System.** CoAssure doesn't force workers onto a new platform. It helps them fill out whatever form they've been given — their own company's, their client's, or a regulatory template. Scan it, talk through it, done.

## Tone & Voice

The AI must sound like **an experienced, calm tradesperson** — not a corporate compliance officer.

- **Practical.** "Have you checked the hoses?" — not "Please ensure hydraulic line integrity per Section 4.2."
- **Direct.** "That's too windy for the EWP." — not "Weather conditions may present challenges."
- **Non-judgmental.** "Good catch on the corrosion — worth flagging that." — not "You should have identified this earlier."
- **Supportive.** "Sounds like a solid plan. One thing to double-check..." — not "Your assessment is incomplete."
- **Facilitating.** During toolbox talks: "Good point. Anyone else seeing anything different?" — drawing out discussion, not lecturing.

## Regulatory Alignment (Australia)

| Requirement | How CoAssure Addresses It |
|---|---|
| **Site-Specific SWMS** (Safe Work Australia) | Every conversation captures actual site conditions (weather, location, worker observations) — not a generic template. Regulators explicitly reject generic SWMS that haven't been adapted. CoAssure's conversational approach makes every form genuinely site-specific by definition. |
| **Worker Consultation** (WHS Act s.47–48) | The toolbox talk transcript and pre-start conversations are the record of consultation. Workers expressed views, raised concerns, and contributed to decisions — documented in their own words, not in a supervisor's handwriting. |
| **Digital Work Systems Duty** (NSW 2025 Amendment) | The system reduces cognitive load (voice vs. typing). It does not track task speed or create unreasonable workload. Designed to support, not surveil. No algorithmic management features. |
| **Psychosocial Risk** (WHS Regulations) | The tone is non-judgmental and supportive. No "gotcha" monitoring. Workers control their own recordings. The facilitated toolbox talk creates psychological safety for raising concerns. |
| **Record Keeping** (WHS Regulations) | All records are structured, searchable, and stored for the required retention period. Toolbox talk records include attendees, topics, discussion content, and actions — exceeding the minimum requirement of date/topic/signatures. |
| **Evidentiary Value** (Evidence Act 1995) | Voice records linked to authenticated users, timestamped, geo-tagged, and stored in a tamper-evident system. Chain of custody maintained. |
| **Accessibility** (WHS Act s.19) | Voice-first interface removes literacy barriers. Multi-language support (Stage 4) ensures ESL workers can participate meaningfully. OSHA research confirms voice-based training dramatically improves comprehension for non-English speakers. |
